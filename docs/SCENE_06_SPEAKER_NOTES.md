# Scene 06 speaker notes — The AI-Native Leader

Target duration: approximately 9–10 minutes. Timings are guidance; every state remains presenter-controlled.

## 01 · Abundance inherited — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Preserve the unresolved Scene 05 question. **Delivery:** Say nothing initially; let the room read it. **Argument:** The audience must feel the consequence of Employee 001’s demonstrated capability before hearing a leadership framework. **Pause:** Full silence. **Transition:** “If access to intelligence changes, differentiation changes.”

## 02 · Differentiation — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Reframe scarcity. **Delivery:** Contrast abundant analysis with scarce responsibility. **Argument:** Access to intelligence becomes less differentiating; deciding what matters becomes more valuable. **Transition:** “So what rises in value?”

## 03 · Judgment + Curiosity — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Begin the human-value answer deliberately. **Delivery:** Give each word space. **Argument:** Judgment chooses what matters; curiosity finds the better question. This is not a claim that AI cannot produce judgment-like or curious outputs. **Transition:** “But decisions live among people.”

## 04 · Empathy + Courage — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Add context and action. **Delivery:** Connect empathy to people, not sentimentality. **Argument:** Empathy reads motivation and context; courage acts when evidence cannot remove all uncertainty. **Transition:** “And direction still requires choice and imagination.”

## 05 · Ethics + Imagination — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Complete the constellation. **Delivery:** Avoid listing; connect each concept to leadership behavior. **Argument:** Ethics asks what should be done; imagination creates possibilities worth pursuing. Human differentiation shifts rather than retreats. **Transition:** “The practical test is simpler.”

## 06 · Trust, decide, own — 22 sec

**Purpose:** Compress the human-value thesis. **Delivery:** Stress trust, make, accept. **Argument:** Knowing which answer to trust, what decision to make, and what responsibility to accept becomes increasingly valuable. **Transition:** “That changes the allocation of work.”

## 07 · AI work — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Recall what Employee 001 actually demonstrated. **Delivery:** Treat these as an expanding operating domain. **Argument:** Research, monitoring, synthesis, scenarios, coordination, and bounded execution increasingly move to the digital workforce. **Transition:** “Human work does not vanish.”

## 08 · Human work — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Show where human contribution concentrates. **Delivery:** Describe an operating-model distinction, not an absolute technical boundary. **Argument:** Intent, purpose, relationships, ethics, accountability, and strategic decisions receive more human capacity. **Transition:** “Human work moves upward.”

## 09 · Work moves — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Land the redistribution thesis. **Delivery:** Pause before “creates value.” **Argument:** AI does not remove the human; it changes where the human creates value. **Pause:** One beat after the final line. **Transition:** “That is why the familiar phrase is no longer enough.”

## 10 · Human in the Loop? — 17 sec

**Purpose:** Challenge passive oversight language. **Delivery:** Ask rather than dismiss. **Argument:** Human in the Loop can reduce a person to a checkpoint inside a machine-designed workflow. **Transition:** “The ambition is larger.”

## 11 · Human in Command — 23 sec

**Purpose:** State the governing philosophy. **Delivery:** Let the title resolve, then name intent, boundaries, accountability, purpose, and strategic judgment. **Argument:** AI can perform growing amounts of work while humans remain responsible for the system and its outcomes. **Pause:** Hold on Human in Command. **Transition:** “So what is the leader’s job?”

## 12 · The leader’s job — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Open the leadership shift. **Delivery:** Ask and stop. Keep Employee 001 visible as the cause of the question. **Argument:** Leadership changes because capability has changed. **Pause:** Invite the room to form an answer. **Transition:** “It is not to beat the machine at every task.”

## 13 · Leadership system — 23 sec

**Purpose:** Construct the new leadership work. **Delivery:** Build direction, design, judgment, trust, and orchestration as connected responsibilities. **Argument:** The leader need not become the organization’s best prompt engineer; the leader designs how capability works. **Transition:** “This is orchestration.”

## 14 · Orchestration statement — 23 sec

**Purpose:** Deliver a signature leadership proposition. **Delivery:** Slow down on “orchestration of capability.” **Argument:** Leadership creates coherence among capabilities around intent and outcomes. **Pause:** Brief silence. **Transition:** “Now look at the workforce this leader is orchestrating.”

## 15 · Human workforce — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Establish the first capability domain. **Delivery:** Call these comparative organizational strengths, not exclusive human properties. **Argument:** Empathy, relationships, context, purpose, accountability, and creativity shape shared meaning. **Transition:** “Beside it is a second form of workforce capability.”

## 16 · AI workforce — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Make the digital workforce tangible. **Delivery:** Point back to Employee 001 and specialist 014. **Argument:** Scale, memory, monitoring, analysis, speed, synthesis, and execution are now organizational capacity. **Transition:** “The goal is not two disconnected teams.”

## 17 · One workforce — 22 sec

**Purpose:** Converge the domains. **Delivery:** Describe two forms of capability, not two species in competition. **Argument:** AI expands organizational and human capability when both domains share work intentionally. **Transition:** “Coherence does not happen automatically.”

## 18 · Orchestration layer — 23 sec

**Purpose:** Visualize leadership as a command plane. **Delivery:** Emphasize connection rather than hierarchy. **Argument:** The leader establishes intent, coherence, boundaries, trust, and shared outcomes across the whole system. **Transition:** “That demands a broader kind of understanding.”

## 19 · Future leader, part one — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Begin Felix’s signature statement. **Delivery:** Reveal only the first half. **Argument:** Understanding technology alone is not the differentiator; leaders must understand people and digital employees. **Transition:** Continue without commentary.

## 20 · Future leader, complete — 27 sec

**Purpose:** Complete the central statement. **Delivery:** Land “blend them together” with conviction. **Argument:** Human intelligence brings motivation, relationships, experience, values, and meaning; artificial intelligence brings scale, memory, analysis, and autonomous capability. Leadership combines both deliberately. **Pause:** Hold the complete statement. **Transition:** “Not two teams.”

## 21 · One team — 17 sec

**Purpose:** Resolve the hybrid workforce architecture. **Delivery:** Keep it restrained. **Argument:** Shared intent and a shared outcome turn separate capabilities into one team. **Transition:** “And that changes the mathematics.”

## 22 · Human + AI — 17 sec

**Purpose:** Establish the additive model. **Delivery:** Call it useful but incremental. **Argument:** If humans and AI merely work beside each other, the result is added productivity. **Transition:** “AI-native design asks for more.”

## 23 · Human × AI — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Transform addition into multiplication. **Delivery:** Let the symbol change carry the argument. **Argument:** AI amplifies human capability; human intent, context, judgment, and meaning make AI capability more valuable. **Transition:** “That is the AI-native difference.”

## 24 · Multiplication equation — 20 sec

**Purpose:** Connect to Scene 02. **Delivery:** Do not over-explain the equation. **Argument:** Redesigning the work allows each form of intelligence to change what the other can do: using AI is not being AI-native. **Pause:** Brief silence. **Transition:** “Now apply the Day-1 question to the organization.”

## 25 · Organization on Day 1 — 27 sec

**Purpose:** Bring the narrative full circle. **Delivery:** Ask slowly and allow the question to remain unanswered. **Argument:** The North Star expands from process design to organizational design. **Pause:** Full silence. **Transition:** “And we will not answer that with theory alone.”

## 26 · Theory informs — 19 sec

**Purpose:** Introduce Felix’s education philosophy. **Delivery:** Respect theory while naming its limit. **Argument:** Another hundred pages can orient us, but reading cannot make an organization AI-native. **Transition:** “Experience does something different.”

## 27 · Experience transforms — 25 sec

**Purpose:** Complete the philosophy. **Delivery:** Connect it to what the audience just experienced. **Argument:** We become AI-native by working with AI on real problems, discovering surprises and failures, and redesigning around what we learn. **Pause:** Hold the complete phrase. **Transition:** “Which leaves one practical question.”

## 28 · Monday cliffhanger — 22 sec

**Purpose:** Create forward energy into Scene 07 without answering it. **Delivery:** Ask conversationally and stop. **Argument:** Transformation becomes real through action, not agreement. **Pause:** Cut before offering the challenge. **Transition:** Scene 07 will own the Monday Morning Challenge.
