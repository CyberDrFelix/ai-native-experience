## Opening and readiness — 1:19

Let Scene 03’s question hang, then answer simply: “If AI is an employee, onboard it like one.” Humans receive onboarding because capability without context is not enough. Intelligence alone is not organizational readiness. Culture, Constitution and Impact are operating architecture, not HR paperwork.

Optional callback: the Digital Felix hand gestures were not a random defect; the twin learned demonstrated behavior from its training. Good onboarding makes context and expected behavior explicit instead of assuming intelligence will infer them correctly.

## Culture — 1:51

An AI employee must understand where it works. Values and expected behavior cannot be inferred from intelligence. “Who are we?” provides identity, “What are we trying to achieve?” provides direction, and “What does good look like here?” makes quality contextual. We teach people for weeks, yet often give AI one prompt and expect understanding. Culture improves usefulness; trust still requires boundaries.

## Constitution — 3:54

Governance should enable useful autonomy, not eliminate it. Start with Permission, then Approval, Prohibition, Escalation and Data. Different work deserves different autonomy; escalation is a designed handoff, not failure.

For the fictional potentially material issue, ask the room before exposing the choices. Select A/B/C from the keyboard or click. A maximizes speed before validation; C maximizes control but creates a bottleneck; B balances investigation and accountable approval for this scenario. B is not universal—the boundary depends on materiality, risk, reversibility, confidence, governance and policy. Good governance defines where AI may act; it does not prevent action everywhere.

## Impact and performance — 3:04

An AI employee needs a job, not novelty. Pause on its first 30 days. Helpful means improving the whole team. Knowledge requires evidence, context, validation and appropriate sources. Thinking outside the box means imagining a better process instead of reproducing the old one faster. Return explicitly to the North Star.

Efficiency matters, but time saved is incomplete. Ask whether quality, speed, insight, creativity, decisions and human capacity improved: did the organization become more capable?

## Activation and bridge — 1:52

Context, boundaries and expectations are now embedded. Employee 001 crosses from capable to ready and active. Ask “What did we just do?” and pause. Land the signature insight with an optimistic, pragmatic tone: we onboarded a member of the workforce. Employee 001 begins monitoring; cut on the anomaly without revealing Scene 05.
